01 / THE QUESTION

Three inequalities. One social outcome.

Students enter the same institution with different starting conditions. This project treats inequality not as one variable, but as three connected dimensions.

The question is not simply whether inequality exists. It is whether campus life reproduces these differences, reduces them, or does both depending on the type of social activity.

A

Spending power

Can a student afford the meals, cafés, travel and outings through which some friendships are built?

B

Prior exposure

How familiar is a student with clubs, mixed social groups, competitions, travel and unfamiliar social situations?

C

Regional context

How different does campus social life feel from the language, norms and social context a student came from?

04 / PATTERNS

Where inequality remains, and where campus life appears to narrow it.

SPENDING POWER

Paid vs free participation

If the gap is much larger for paid activities than free activities, cost is shaping participation rather than social motivation alone.

PRIOR EXPOSURE

Initial vs current comfort

The gap in initial comfort is large. Current comfort improves across groups, showing adaptation without complete equalization.

REGIONAL CONTEXT

How social comfort changes after entering college

The comparison is descriptive. Region is a proxy for context, not a causal explanation for any individual student.

05 / METHOD

From questionnaire to network.

The research design is built backwards from the visualizations. Every survey variable exists because it answers part of the central question or creates an interpretable network edge.

  1. Measure starting conditions.Region type, spending power, prior exposure, initial social comfort and context gap.
  2. Measure social access.Frequency of participation in ten campus and off-campus activity types.
  3. Measure outcomes.Current comfort, friend-group diversity, social circle size and belonging.
  4. Construct the network.Each student is connected to every activity they report participating in. Frequency becomes edge weight.
  5. Compare groups.Paid/free participation by spending power, social comfort by exposure and region, and adaptation by year.
Important limitation

The bundled dataset is synthetic and exists only to demonstrate the workflow. It can be replaced with real anonymous responses using the same column names. Findings on this page therefore demonstrate what the method can reveal; they are not empirical claims about a real student population.

07 / WHAT THIS MODEL SUGGESTS

College can be an equalizer without being an equal starting point.

In the synthetic model, spending power continues to shape access to paid social spaces, and students with more prior exposure begin with greater social comfort. At the same time, widely accessible campus activities are connected across spending groups and comfort improves with time. The useful question is therefore not “does inequality disappear?” but “which parts of campus life reproduce it, and which parts help bridge it?”
